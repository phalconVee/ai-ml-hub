# Kestrel Field Station: Operations Handbook

Kestrel is a fictional high-altitude research station used as sample data for this project.

## Power

The station runs on a solar array of 48 panels backed by a 120 kWh battery bank. In winter the array
produces about a third of its summer output, so the night shift switches the greenhouse lights to the
reduced schedule: lights on from 06:00 to 18:00 only. If the battery falls below 20 percent, the duty
engineer starts the backup methanol fuel cell and logs the start in the power book.

## Water

Water comes from snowmelt collected in two 8,000-litre tanks. Every Monday the duty engineer tests the
tanks for nitrates and turbidity. A turbidity reading above 4 NTU means the UV filter cartridge must be
replaced before anyone drinks from that tank again. Spare cartridges are kept in the blue cabinet in the
workshop.

## Visitors

Visiting researchers must book a bunk at least ten days ahead through the station coordinator. Visits
are limited to 21 days. Everyone completes the altitude briefing on their first morning, and nobody
hikes above the north ridge alone.

## Emergencies

The satellite phone is charged in the radio room and tested every Friday at 12:00. The nearest clinic is
in Varn, a four-hour drive in good weather. Helicopter evacuation is possible only when the wind at the
helipad stays below 35 km/h.
